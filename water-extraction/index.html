Standing water in a Lima home or business is an emergency, and the water extraction line is open 24/7. Water extraction means pumping and vacuuming liquid water out of a structure so drying can begin, and it has more effect on how much of the building survives than any step that follows. Every hour water sits, it wicks further into drywall, subfloors and contents.

While you wait for a crew, stop the source if you can do so safely. If you cannot find the leak, close the main water valve. Keep power off to wet areas unless the panel is dry and easy to reach, and lift electronics, papers and furniture legs out of the water.

When Standing Water Is An Emergency

Any water above the floor surface that is still spreading is an emergency, but a few situations cannot wait until morning. Water near outlets, the panel or a plugged-in appliance is one. Water that came from a sewer drain or from outdoors is another, because it was never clean to begin with.

Water that is still rising, standing in a finished basement or soaking carpet over a pad also belongs here. Padding holds water like a sponge, and the longer it stays saturated the less likely it is to be worth keeping. Same-day and after-hours response is offered when crews are available.

How Water Is Extracted

The method depends on how much water there is and what it is standing on. Deep water goes first, then the thin film that hides in carpet fibers and along baseboards.

  • Submersible pumps move several inches of water out of basements, garages and crawl spaces.
  • Portable extraction units pull water from carpet and padding at the surface.
  • Weighted wands press water out of carpet and flat hard floors in passes across the room.
  • Moisture meters and thermal imaging confirm what is still wet once the visible water is gone.

Moisture meters matter because water that has left the floor surface is often still inside wall cavities and under flooring. Extraction removes liquid water only. What remains in the materials is handled by structural drying and dehumidification, which usually starts the same day.

Water Categories And What They Change

The source of the water decides how it can be handled. This table summarizes the three categories used in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.

CategoryTypical sourceEffect on extraction
Category 1Supply line, tub overflow, clean rainwaterExtract and dry. Most materials can often be saved.
Category 2Washing machine or dishwasher dischargeExtract and dry with more care. Porous materials may be removed.
Category 3Sewage, river or ground floodingContaminated. Porous materials usually come out. See sewage backup cleanup.

Water often moves up a category as it sits, so a clean leak left for days can need the same caution as gray water.

Flooded Basements, Crawl Spaces And Whole-House Water

Large volumes of water change the job. A flooded basement needs pumping at a controlled rate, because emptying it too quickly can stress walls that are holding back wet soil. Our basement water damage cleanup page explains that sequence.

Crawl spaces are tight and hard to dry, so the work focuses on getting standing water out before insulation and joists absorb it, as described on the crawl space water damage page. When several rooms or floors are involved after a river or storm event, the plan follows the steps on the flood damage cleanup page.

Extraction In Lima: Rain, Rivers And Older Basements

Lima sits on the Ottawa River, and local reporting in June 2015 described flooding in Lima and Ada after four to six inches of rain fell in one night. For a property owner, the lesson is that water can arrive from the ground and the storm drain at the same time, so ask whether your policy covers each source separately.

About 60 percent of the city's sewer system is combined sewer, according to the EPA's summary of its 2014 Clean Water Act settlement. If water came up through a floor drain, treat it as contaminated and read the sewage backup page before touching anything.

Standards Worth Knowing

Standing water is removed first, and the same ANSI/IICRC S500 standard that guides professional water damage restoration is a fair benchmark to hold any crew to.

What Affects The Cost Of Water Extraction

Extraction is usually the smallest part of a water loss bill, but the numbers still vary with the property. These are the factors that set the scope.

Depth and volume

More water means larger pumps, longer run times and more labor.

Water category

Contaminated water adds protective gear, disposal and removal of porous materials.

Access and layout

Stairs, finished basements and tight spaces slow the work.

Materials affected

Carpet, padding and hardwood each need a different extraction method.

Drying that follows

Most losses continue straight into drying, which is scoped separately.

Homeowners policies often cover sudden water damage from a failed line, and they often exclude flood and groundwater. Read your policy before you assume either way.

Common Questions

Do you handle water extraction emergencies at night or on weekends in Lima?

Yes. The line is answered 24/7, including holidays, and same-day response is offered when crews are available. Tell the dispatcher how deep the water is and where it came from so the right equipment goes out.

Can I remove the water myself with a wet vacuum?

A shop vacuum helps with a small spill, but it cannot keep up with an inch of water across a room, and it leaves the water inside carpet pad and wall bases. Never use a vacuum near wet outlets or if the water touched electrical equipment.

Does extraction dry my floors?

No. Extraction removes liquid water. The moisture that soaked into subfloors, drywall and framing is removed with air movers and dehumidifiers during structural drying.

How long does extraction take?

A small room can take under an hour, while a flooded basement can take much of a day. The crew will give you a plan once they see the depth and the layout.

Will I need to move out while the water is removed?

Usually not for clean water. If the water is contaminated or power must be off in the affected area, a short stay elsewhere may be the safer choice.
