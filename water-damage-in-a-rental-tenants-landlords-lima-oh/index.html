Water Damage in a Rental: Tenants and Landlords in Lima, OH

Both sides have duties after a leak. Here is how to handle it clearly and calmly.

By the Lima Water Damage Pros team · 2026

Water damage in a rental affects two parties with different duties. Tenants should report the problem quickly and protect their belongings. Landlords must make repairs and keep the unit habitable. This guide explains the basics for Lima, with Ohio law in plain language.

This is general information and not legal advice. Local legal aid or an attorney can answer questions about a specific lease.

What Ohio Law Says About Landlords

Ohio Revised Code section 5321.04 sets landlord duties. They include complying with building and housing codes, making repairs needed to keep the unit fit and habitable, and keeping plumbing and other facilities in good working order.

Landlords must also supply running water and a reasonable amount of heat. Water damage can touch several of these duties at once.

What Tenants Should Do

  1. Stop the water if you can safely do so, and tell the landlord right away.
  2. Put the report in writing, by text or email, and keep a copy.
  3. Photograph the damage and your belongings.
  4. Move valuables to a dry place.
  5. Ask the landlord what they plan to do and when.
  6. Check your own renter's policy for belongings coverage.

What Landlords Should Do

Act quickly. Shut off the source, call a plumber if needed and start drying. Delay lets damage spread and weakens your insurance position.

Document the loss, keep the tenant informed and give notice before entry as the lease and law require. See structural drying and dehumidification for how drying is documented.

Multi-Unit And Upstairs Leaks

Water often passes between units. A leak upstairs can damage the ceiling below, and each unit needs its own scope. The commercial water damage restoration page explains how multi-unit losses are mapped.

Clear photographs in each unit make it easier to sort responsibility.

When To Get Outside Help

If the landlord does not respond, contact local legal aid or a tenant advocate. If the unit is unsafe, ask the city code office what steps are available.

Do not withhold rent without legal advice. Ohio has specific procedures for tenants.

Written Notices And Timelines

Put every notice in writing and date it. A text or email counts, and a copy kept on your phone or computer protects you.

Include what happened, when you noticed it, what you did and what you are asking the other side to do. Keep the tone factual.

If you receive a reply, save it. A clear paper trail helps if the matter goes to a landlord, an insurer or a court.

Protecting Belongings And Moving Out Temporarily

Move valuables, electronics and papers to a dry place and photograph them. Renter's insurance often covers belongings, and photographs support the claim.

If the unit is unsafe, ask in writing about temporary housing. Leases and the law differ on who pays, so get advice.

Keep receipts for hotel stays, laundry and storage. Ask your insurer which expenses it reimburses.

Common Disputes And How To Avoid Them

Disputes often start with delay. A tenant reports a leak, the landlord is slow to respond and damage spreads. Prompt written reports and prompt action prevent most of that.

Another dispute is over belongings. Renter's insurance is the standard answer, and tenants should carry it.

A third is over access. Landlords need to enter to repair, and tenants need notice. Agree on times in writing.

If you cannot agree, a local mediator or legal aid office may help before the dispute becomes expensive.

Staying Practical

Most water problems in rentals are resolved without conflict when both sides act quickly and communicate in writing. The unit gets dried, belongings are replaced and everyone moves on.

If a problem drags on, document each delay and ask for a response by a specific date. Keep the tone calm.

Seek advice early if safety or habitability is at risk.

Before you sign a lease, read the sections on repairs, entry and renter's insurance. Knowing the terms in advance makes a future leak much less stressful, because you will already know whom to call and what to put in writing.

Rentals In Lima And Ada

Lima and Ada both have large rental markets, with Ada tied closely to Ohio Northern University. Student renters in older houses are often the first to notice a leak, so reporting it in writing early helps everyone.

Questions About This Topic

Who pays for my belongings after a rental leak?

Often renter's insurance, unless the lease says otherwise. Check your policy.

Can I call a restoration company myself?

Ask the landlord first, since they usually control the work. In an emergency, protect the unit and tell them what you did.

Does the landlord have to repair water damage?

Under Ohio law, landlords must make repairs needed to keep the unit habitable. Ask legal aid about specifics.

Do I still owe rent?

Ask a legal professional. Do not withhold rent without advice.

What if mold appears?

Report it in writing. Mold remediation is a separate trade from water damage restoration.
