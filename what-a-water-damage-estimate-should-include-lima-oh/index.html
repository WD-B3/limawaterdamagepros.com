What a Water Damage Estimate Should Include in Lima, OH

A good estimate is specific. Here is a checklist of what it should show and what to question.

By the Lima Water Damage Pros team · 2026

A water damage estimate should tell you what will be done, where, with what and for how much. Vague totals with no detail are a warning sign. This guide lists what a Lima homeowner should expect to see on a written estimate.

A number quoted before anyone has seen the property is a guess. A good estimate follows an inspection and moisture readings.

Scope Of Work

The scope lists each room, the area affected and the work planned. It should say which materials will be removed, dried or replaced.

Look for square footage and clear descriptions rather than just room names.

A Quick Checklist

Line itemWhat it should sayRed flag
Water categoryClean, gray or blackNot mentioned
ExtractionMethod and areaLump sum with no area
EquipmentNumber of air movers and dehumidifiers and daysJust "equipment"
MonitoringDaily readings includedNo readings
DemolitionMaterials removed and whyRemove everything
RepairPriced separately from dryingBundled with no detail

If any of these are missing, ask for them in writing before you sign.

Equipment And Drying Days

Drying equipment is usually priced by the piece and the day. The estimate should show how many pieces and an expected number of days, with a note that readings decide the final count.

Our structural drying and dehumidification page explains how equipment is placed and removed.

Separate Drying From Repair

Drying is the emergency phase, and repair is the rebuild. They are often priced separately, and the repair scope may change after readings show what can be saved.

An estimate that bundles both with no detail makes comparison hard. Ask for a split estimate.

Questions To Ask Before You Sign

  1. Is this an estimate or a fixed price?
  2. What could change the total?
  3. Who pulls permits, if any are needed?
  4. How will you handle hidden damage found during demolition?
  5. Will you share daily readings and photographs?
  6. What is not included?

A contractor who welcomes these questions is a better partner than one who rushes you.

Comparing Estimates

Compare line by line rather than by total. A lower number with less equipment or no monitoring is not the same job.

If the work is roof or ceiling related, see how ceiling water damage repair is scoped. The scope and price should be explained before work starts.

Why Estimates Change During The Job

An estimate is based on what can be seen at the start. Behind walls and under floors, conditions can differ, and the scope may grow or shrink.

A good estimate explains how changes will be handled. It should say who approves added work, how price changes are documented and what happens if drying finishes sooner than planned.

Be cautious about any estimate that promises it cannot change. Water damage has unknowns, and honesty about them is a good sign.

Estimates Versus Insurance Estimates

Your insurer may write its own estimate, which can differ from the restoration company estimate. Differences often come from line items, unit prices or missing work.

Share both documents with each side and ask for explanations. Many differences are resolved by adding readings or photographs.

Do not sign a contract until you understand how the two estimates interact. Ask who is responsible if the insurer pays less than the invoice.

Questions About Subcontractors And Warranties

Ask whether the same company will do the repair or bring in other trades. Subcontracted work should be described, and you should know who stands behind it.

Ask about warranties on repair work and on materials. Get the terms in writing.

Ask how long the estimate is valid, since material prices change.

Ask what happens if the insurer pays less than the estimate. Knowing your responsibility beforehand avoids a surprise at the end.

Putting It To Use

Use the checklist when you receive your first estimate and again when you receive the second. Mark each missing item and ask the company to fill it in.

A short email with your questions creates a record and gives the company a chance to respond clearly. Compare the answers side by side.

Choose the estimate that you understand best, not the one with the smallest number.

If an estimate arrives as a single page with one total, ask for the detailed version. Detail is what lets you compare companies fairly, and it is what your insurer will ask for anyway. A company that cannot produce it is telling you something about how it works.

Keep every version of the estimate, because the changes between versions tell the story of the job and show what was added, removed or revised.

Estimates For Older Lima Homes

Older homes in Lima may have plaster walls, old plumbing and layered flooring. These can raise the scope, and a good estimate allows for hidden conditions.

Questions About This Topic

Does an estimate cost anything?

Practices differ. Ask what is included in the estimate and whether any fee applies before you agree.

Why does the final cost change?

Hidden damage and extra drying days can change the scope. A good estimate explains how.

Is a phone quote reliable?

Not for a specific loss. Someone should see the property.

What if two estimates differ a lot?

Compare scope, equipment and monitoring. The difference is usually in the detail.

Does my insurer need the estimate?

Often yes. Ask what format they require.
