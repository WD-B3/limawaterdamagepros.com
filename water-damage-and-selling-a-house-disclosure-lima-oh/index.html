Water Damage and Selling a House: Disclosure in Lima, OH

Past water damage does not have to block a sale, but it does need honest handling. Here is how disclosure works in Ohio.

By the Lima Water Damage Pros team · 2026

If your Lima home has had water damage, you will likely need to say so when you sell. Ohio law uses a standard disclosure form, and past leaks, repairs and insurance claims are common questions on it. This guide explains the basics in plain language.

We are not lawyers, and this is not legal advice. Your real estate agent or attorney can tell you how the rules apply to your sale.

The Ohio Disclosure Form

Ohio Revised Code section 5302.30 requires most sellers of residential property to complete a disclosure form. It asks about the structure, water supply, sewer and other conditions the seller knows about.

The statute includes a list of exemptions, so not every sale requires the form. Ask your agent or attorney whether your transaction does.

Answer based on what you actually know. If you are unsure, say so rather than guess.

Documenting Repairs

Keep invoices, the scope of work, photographs and daily moisture readings from the restoration company. They show the damage was found, dried and repaired.

Permit records matter too. See our guide on wall and drywall water damage repair and the permit article on this blog for how repairs and records fit together.

How Buyers And Inspectors React

Inspectors look for stains, odors and moisture readings. A documented repair is usually easier to accept than an unexplained patch.

If an inspector finds moisture, you may be asked for further testing or a credit. Fixing the cause of the problem before listing helps.

Fix Before You List, Or Disclose And Adjust

You can repair the damage and disclose the repair, or sell as is with a clear disclosure and a price that reflects it. Each choice has trade-offs for time, cost and risk.

Talk with your agent about the local market. An incomplete repair, such as paint over a wet wall, is the worst of both choices.

If The Loss Is Fresh

If damage just happened, get it dried and documented first. The structural drying and dehumidification page explains the process, and readings can show the structure reached a dry state.

For larger losses, scope and photographs matter even more. A buyer will often ask for them.

Talking To Buyers And Agents

Be direct and organized. Hand over a folder with the scope, photographs, readings and invoices, and let the documents answer most questions.

Buyers often worry less about a past leak that was dried and repaired than about one that was hidden. Clear records turn a worry into a closed topic.

Let your agent handle price discussions, and ask them how local buyers tend to react to repaired water damage.

When The Damage Is Not Repaired Yet

If you sell before repairs are done, disclose the damage and the status clearly. Buyers may ask for a price adjustment or a repair credit.

Get written estimates for the remaining work, so the credit is based on numbers rather than guesses.

An attorney can explain how contract language handles known defects. Do not rely on a casual promise at a showing.

Pre-Listing Checklist

Gather repair invoices, scopes, readings and photographs into one folder. Include the name of the contractor and the dates of work.

Walk the house with a moisture meter or hire an inspector before listing. Finding a small issue early is better than having a buyer find it.

Fix any ongoing leak, and make sure there is no musty smell. Buyers notice smells quickly.

Ask your agent how to word disclosure answers, and keep a copy of the signed form.

Keeping Records For The Next Owner

Even after the sale, keep a copy of your disclosure and repair records. Questions can come up years later, and documents answer them.

If you are buying, ask for the same records from the seller. A folder with invoices and readings is a good sign that the repair was done carefully.

Store copies digitally so they survive another leak.

If you inherited the house or bought it recently, say what you know and what you do not. Disclosure covers your actual knowledge, so gather the records you received at closing and any inspection reports. They may answer questions that you cannot answer from memory.

Selling In Lima And Nearby Towns

Buyers in the Lima area may ask about basement dampness, sewer backups and past flooding, since heavy rain in June 2015 affected parts of Lima and Ada. Honest, documented answers are easier to handle than surprises at inspection.

If your home is in a nearby town, our pages for Ada and Delphos describe local conditions.

Questions About This Topic

Do I have to disclose past water damage?

The Ohio disclosure form asks about conditions you know of. Ask your agent or attorney how it applies to you.

Can I sell a house with water damage?

Yes, with proper disclosure. Many homes with past water damage sell.

Should I repair before listing?

Often easier to market, but it depends on cost and timing. Talk to your agent.

What if I forgot about a past leak?

Check your records and ask your agent. Honesty helps.

Are there exemptions to the form?

Yes. ORC 5302.30 lists exemptions. Ask your agent.
