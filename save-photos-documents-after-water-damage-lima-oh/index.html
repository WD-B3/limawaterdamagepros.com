How to Save Photos and Documents After Water Damage in Lima, OH

Paper and photos can often be saved if you act quickly and gently. Here is how to handle them.

By the Lima Water Damage Pros team · 2026

Wet photographs, certificates, tax records and keepsakes are often the hardest losses after a leak. Many can be saved if you handle them gently and start drying within a day or two. This guide covers the basic steps for Lima homeowners and when to stop and call a document recovery specialist.

Never pull stuck sheets apart. Wet paper tears easily, and photographs can lose their image when peeled.

Move Them Out Of The Water First

Lift wet items from the water by supporting them from below. Carry papers on a flat tray or cookie sheet rather than by the edges.

If the water was contaminated, wear gloves, and treat the items as dirty. Clean water and contaminated water call for different handling.

Air Drying Papers And Books

  1. Lay single sheets flat on clean towels or blotting paper in a cool room.
  2. Change the towels as they absorb water.
  3. Stand books on their tops with pages fanned slightly if only the edges are wet.
  4. For soaked books and thick files, interleave sheets with paper towel.
  5. Use a fan on low across the room, not directly on the papers.

Photographs

Rinse mud or dirt gently in clean water, then lay photos face up on a clean surface to dry. Keep them from touching each other.

Old prints and negatives are more fragile than modern prints. If they are irreplaceable, a conservator can help.

Freezing stops mold growth and buys time. A freezer is a holding step, not a repair, and a specialist can freeze-dry items later.

Documents You Need To Replace

Birth certificates, titles and deeds can usually be replaced through the issuing office. Keep a list of what was damaged and photograph the originals before drying.

For tax records, ask your tax preparer what is needed. Copies stored digitally are the easiest long-term protection, and the next water loss will feel smaller if you have them.

Preventing The Next Loss

Store important papers off the floor in a waterproof container, and scan them to a cloud folder or external drive. Avoid storing paper in basements or against exterior walls.

If the loss came from a leak you have not yet traced, the burst pipe water damage and basement water damage cleanup pages cover common sources.

What A Specialist Can Do

Document recovery firms use vacuum freeze drying and other methods to dry books, photographs and records. These methods are slower and cost more than home drying, so they are best for items that cannot be replaced.

Freeze the items first and call the specialist the next business day. Freezing buys you time and stops mold from developing while you decide.

Ask what the firm can recover, how long it takes and what it costs before you hand over the material. Get the answers in writing.

Digitizing For Next Time

Once documents are dry, scan them. A phone scanning app is enough for most papers, and a flatbed scanner gives better results for photographs.

Store the scans in two places, such as an external drive and a cloud folder. Label them clearly so a family member could find them in an emergency.

Keep originals that matter legally, such as deeds and certificates, in a fire and water resistant box on an upper floor.

What Not To Do With Wet Paper

Do not use heat, an iron or a microwave to dry paper. Heat can bake in stains and ruin delicate inks.

Do not pull apart pages that are stuck unless they separate easily. Forcing them tears the surface.

Do not store damp papers in sealed plastic at room temperature. Mold can start in a day or two, so either dry them or freeze them.

Do not throw away anything of legal importance until you have checked how to replace it.

Priorities When You Have A Lot Of Paper

If many items are wet, start with the ones that cannot be replaced. Deeds, wills, original photographs and family records come before bills and magazines.

Freeze what you cannot dry in time, and sort the rest into dry now and discard piles. Photograph anything you discard so you can list it later.

Ask family members which items matter most to them.

Questions About This Topic

Can wet photos be saved?

Often yes if they are not allowed to dry stuck together. Gentle rinsing and air drying help.

Should I use a hair dryer?

No. Heat can warp paper and photos. Use low airflow and time.

How long do I have?

Start within one or two days. After that, mold becomes a risk, which is why freezing is useful.

Can I save wet electronics media?

Do not plug them in. A specialist can recover data in some cases.

Can wet passports be replaced?

Yes, but contact the issuing agency. Keep the damaged document.
