Structural Drying in Lima, OH

Drying is a measured process. Learn how equipment is placed, what readings mean and how long drying usually takes.

Structural drying and dehumidification is the process of removing moisture from walls, floors, ceilings and framing after water has soaked them. In Lima, where winter heating dries indoor air and summer humidity stays high, drying works only when the equipment and airflow suit the conditions. This page explains how it is planned, measured and finished.

If a loss just happened, stop the source, remove standing water and open doors between wet and dry areas only if you were told to. Do not run household fans on wet drywall without guidance, because they can spread moisture.

What Structural Drying Means

Water you can see is only part of the problem. Wet drywall, framing and subfloor hold water inside, and surface drying does not reach it.

Structural drying pulls moisture out of materials and removes it from the air, so the building returns to its normal moisture content. It is a controlled process, not a matter of leaving fans on.

Equipment Used

Air movers

High-velocity fans move air across wet surfaces to carry moisture away from them.

Dehumidifiers

They remove moisture from the air so wet materials keep releasing water.

Moisture meters

Meters read moisture in drywall, wood and concrete so progress can be tracked.

Thermal cameras

They show temperature differences that point to wet areas behind surfaces.

How Drying Is Planned

  1. Map the wet area with meters and a thermal camera.
  2. Set dry reference readings from unaffected areas of the same material.
  3. Place air movers and dehumidifiers based on the size and layout of the space.
  4. Take daily readings at the same points.
  5. Adjust equipment as areas dry.
  6. Remove equipment only when readings match the reference.

If standing water remains, it must come out first, as explained on the water extraction page.

How Long Does Drying Take?

Most drying runs several days. Dense materials like hardwood, plaster and concrete take longer than carpet or drywall.

Equipment stays until the readings say the structure is dry, not until a number of days passes. Ask to see the readings if you are unsure.

Water Mitigation And Why It Matters

Water mitigation is the emergency work that limits how far water spreads and how much it damages, including extraction, moving contents and starting drying. Mitigation comes first and drying follows, and both happen before any rebuild.

Good mitigation keeps a small job small. Waiting a day or two lets water move into wall cavities and under flooring, and it widens the repair.

How Much Equipment Will I Need?

Equipment depends on room size, ceiling height, the materials wet and the water category. A common way to size the job is by cubic area and wet surface, but the exact count is set by readings, not a formula alone.

Dehumidifiers and air movers draw power, so expect your electric bill to rise during drying. That is normal, and a written scope should list what is on site. Keep the doors to the drying area closed.

What Drying Cannot Fix

Drying cannot save materials that have already broken down. Soaked insulation, heavily saturated particleboard and contaminated carpet are usually replaced. Drying also does not stop a leak, so the source has to be repaired first.

Pages for specific materials explain these decisions, including carpet and padding and walls and drywall.

Standards Worth Knowing

Reference readings and drying goals in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard are the usual benchmark for deciding when a structure is dry.

What Affects The Cost Of Structural Drying

Drying cost follows the equipment and the days it takes.

Affected area

More rooms and floors mean more equipment.

Materials

Dense materials need more days.

Equipment count

Air movers and dehumidifiers are typically charged per day.

Monitoring

Daily visits to take readings take labor.

Water category

Contaminated water changes what can be dried in place.

Insurers usually cover drying when the water damage itself is covered. Keep the daily readings, because they document that the work was needed and complete.

Common Questions

How long does drying equipment need to run?

Several days is typical. The readings decide, and the crew removes equipment once the structure matches dry reference points.

Why is equipment so loud and warm?

Air movers and dehumidifiers run hard to pull moisture out. Warmth from dehumidifiers is normal.

Can I use my own fans and dehumidifier?

Household equipment usually cannot dry wet framing and subfloor. It can help in a minor, surface-level spill.

Will drying stop mold?

Fast, thorough drying lowers the chance of mold growing. If you see mold, a mold specialist should assess it.

What if readings are still high after several days?

The crew may adjust the equipment or open a wall cavity to reach trapped moisture.
