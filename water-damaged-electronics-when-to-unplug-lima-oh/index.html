Water-Damaged Electronics: When to Unplug in Lima, OH

Water and power do not mix. Here is how to decide what to unplug, what to leave and when to call someone.

By the Lima Water Damage Pros team · 2026

Water and electricity create a real risk of shock, so the order of operations matters. This guide explains what to unplug, what to leave alone and what to check before you turn anything back on after a leak in Lima.

Your safety comes first. If water has reached outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, do not wade in. Call the utility or 911 about any electrical risk before you do anything else.

Do Not Step Into Standing Water To Reach A Plug

Standing water can carry current from a damaged outlet or appliance. If you cannot reach the item with dry feet, do not reach it.

If the main panel is dry and the path is safe, you can shut off the breaker for the wet area. If not, call the utility.

What To Unplug

DeviceActionNotes
Small appliancesUnplug if the floor is dryDo not turn on until inspected
Computers and TVsUnplug and move to a dry roomDo not power up
Power stripsUnplug if dry to reachReplace if wet
Large appliancesLeave unplugged and call a technicianWater in motors is a risk
Lamps and cordsUnplug, inspect for moistureReplace damaged cords

If you are unsure, leave it unplugged and ask an electrician.

What To Leave For An Electrician

Wet outlets, switches and panels should be inspected by a licensed electrician before they are used again. Water inside a wall can reach wiring without any visible sign.

City of Lima building department permits cover electrical work, according to a city notice reported by local media. Ask your electrician about permits and inspections for any repair beyond a simple replacement.

Drying Electronics

  1. Unplug the device and remove batteries.
  2. Wipe the exterior with a dry cloth.
  3. Place it in a dry, ventilated spot for several days.
  4. Do not use a hair dryer, which can push water deeper.
  5. Have a technician inspect it before it is powered on.

Rice is a myth for serious water exposure. Time and technician inspection do more.

Why Air Movers Need Care Too

Restoration crews run air movers and dehumidifiers on dedicated circuits to avoid overloading outlets. A crew checks that circuits are safe before plugging in equipment.

If the loss is large, ask the crew how power is being supplied. The structural drying page explains the equipment, and its power draw is why your electric bill rises during drying.

Electronics And Contaminated Water

If the water was sewage or flood, treat electronics as contaminated. Do not try to clean them yourself. A technician can tell you whether repair is possible.

The steps on the flood damage restoration page describe how contents are sorted after contaminated water.

Surge Protectors, Cords And Small Appliances

Surge protectors that were wet should be replaced, not dried and reused. Water can corrode the contacts inside, and the damage is not visible.

Extension cords with water in the plug ends are also risky. Replace them rather than test them.

Small appliances such as toasters and coffee makers are inexpensive compared with the risk of a shorted unit. If one was submerged, retire it.

Working With Your Insurer On Electronics

List each wet device with the brand, model, age and a photograph of the label. Insurers often ask for this, and it speeds payment.

Keep the device until the adjuster has seen it or has said you may discard it. Do not power it on to test it, since that can make the damage worse and change the story.

If a device holds data you need, ask a technician about recovery before you throw it away.

Large Appliances And Home Systems

Refrigerators, washers and dryers can be damaged by water at the base. A technician should inspect them before you run them again.

Furnaces and water heaters in wet basements are a safety concern, especially gas units. Do not relight a pilot after flooding.

Sump pump controls and well pump equipment should be checked after a flood. A damaged pump can fail when you need it.

Keep a list of the models and ages of anything that was wet. It supports an insurance claim.

A Safe Restart Routine

When the area is dry and an electrician has cleared the circuits, restart one thing at a time. Plug in a lamp first, then a small device, and watch for sparks, smells or warm cords.

Leave large appliances for a technician to inspect. If a breaker trips, stop and call.

Take your time, since a rushed restart is how a manageable loss becomes a fire.

Lima Utility And Permit Notes

In winter, outages from storms can leave electronics unplugged for long periods, which makes it easy to forget which devices were wet. Keep a list of anything that was in the wet area.

Questions About This Topic

Is it safe to unplug a wet appliance?

Only if you can reach it with dry feet and no standing water near it.

Can I turn off the breaker myself?

If the panel is dry and the path is safe, yes. If not, call the utility.

How long should electronics dry?

Several days in a dry room, then have them checked.

Do I need an electrician for wet outlets?

Yes. Water inside walls and boxes can affect wiring.

Does insurance cover wet electronics?

Often as part of a covered loss. Keep model numbers and photos.
