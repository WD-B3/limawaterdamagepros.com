Water Damage Restoration In Lima, OH: What It Covers

Water damage restoration is the work of removing unwanted water from a building, drying the structure and its contents, and repairing what could not be saved. In Lima, OH, that covers everything from a supply line that let go under a kitchen sink to a basement that took on water after a hard rain. This page explains how the process works, what affects the cost and what to do in the first hours.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published industry standard for professional water damage restoration, and it is fair to ask any company whether its crews work to it. Ask the same question of anyone you call about water damage repair in Lima, including us. You should also expect a written scope and price before work starts, so nothing about your property comes as a surprise.

Water damage emergencies do not keep business hours, so the emergency line is open 24/7. Same-day response is offered when crews are available. If you are standing in water right now, jump to the first 72 hours section below, then call.

Emergency Response

The First 72 Hours After Water Damage In Lima

Water damage is time sensitive. In the first hours the job is to stop the source and get standing water out. Within a day or two, wet drywall, insulation and carpet padding start to break down, and EPA guidance on mold prevention says to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours.

The table shows what usually happens at each stage of a Lima water loss and what you can do to help.

Safety comes before speed. If water has reached outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, stay out of the wet area and call the utility or 911 about any electrical or gas risk before you call us. Never walk through standing water to reach a panel.

Once the area is safe, the right next call is the one that gets drying started. The water extraction page explains how standing water is removed, and structural drying explains the equipment that follows.

Our Process

Water Damage Repair In Lima After The Drying Is Done

Drying is only half of a water loss. Once readings show the structure is dry, the rebuild begins. This is the water damage repair most people picture, and it is priced and scheduled separately from the emergency work.

Here is how a typical Lima job moves from the first call to a finished room.

1

Assess

A moisture map shows which materials are wet, how far water traveled and what category of water it is.

2

Extract

Pumps and extraction units remove standing water so drying can start on the structure.

3

Dry

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings match dry reference points.

4

Repair

Drywall, insulation, flooring and trim are replaced where drying could not save them.

Water mitigation is the term for the early steps that stop a loss from growing, mainly extraction and drying. Air movers push air across wet surfaces, LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air, extraction units remove liquid water and moisture meters show when materials are dry. The full equipment list and drying logic are on the structural drying and dehumidification page.

How long drying takes depends on the materials and how much water they absorbed. Dense materials such as hardwood, plaster and subfloors take longer than carpet or insulation, which is why daily readings matter more than a calendar estimate.

Why Lima Calls Us

Why Lima Buildings Get Water Damage

Lima sits on the Ottawa River in Allen County, with a mix of older neighborhoods, newer subdivisions and a historic downtown. Water gets into buildings here in a handful of recurring ways, and each one has a practical lesson for property owners.

Combined sewers and heavy rain

About 60 percent of Lima's sewer system is combined sewer, where stormwater and household wastewater share a pipe, according to the EPA's summary of the city's 2014 Clean Water Act settlement. The settlement requires new storage and sewer separation to cut overflows to the Ottawa River.

For a homeowner the practical step is to ask your insurer whether your policy covers sewer or drain backup, because standard policies often leave it out.

The Ottawa River and June 2015

Local reporting in June 2015 described flooding in Ada and Lima after four to six inches of rain from the leading edge of Tropical Storm Bill, with area creeks draining toward the Ottawa River.

Floodwater counts as contaminated, so a loss like that follows the Category 2 and 3 steps on the flood damage cleanup page.

Cold winters and pipes

The 1991 to 2020 climate normals put the average January low in Lima near 19 degrees Fahrenheit. Supply lines in unheated garages, exterior walls and crawl spaces are the usual first failures when a hard freeze arrives.

The frozen pipe damage page explains what to shut off and what to photograph.

Older downtown buildings

Downtown Lima includes the National Register listed Barr Hotel, Hotel Argonne and Allen County Courthouse. Buildings of that age tend to have long original plumbing runs and layered roofs, so a leak can travel a long way before it shows.

Owners of older space benefit from a moisture map before cleanup starts, which is part of every commercial water damage job.

Know Before You Call

What Water Damage Restoration Costs In Lima

Restoration pricing depends on the specific loss, so any number quoted before someone has seen the property is a guess. These are the factors that move the scope and the price, and they are the same ones used to write your itemized scope of work.

Water category

Clean water from a supply line is the simplest. Gray water from appliances and black water from sewage or floods require removal of porous materials and extra safety steps.

Affected area

The number of rooms and floors, and how far water traveled into wall cavities and under flooring, sets the amount of equipment and labor.

Materials involved

Hardwood, plaster, insulation and carpet padding respond differently, and some can be dried in place while others must be replaced.

Drying days and equipment

More air movers and dehumidifiers, and more days of monitoring, are needed for dense materials and large structures.

Rebuild scope

Replacing drywall, flooring and trim after drying is priced separately from the emergency and drying work.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by homeowners insurance, gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage usually requires separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer what applies before you decide on repairs.

Questions

Water Damage Restoration FAQs For Lima

How soon should I call after I find water damage in Lima?

Call as soon as you find it. Wet drywall, insulation and padding start to break down within a day or two, so the sooner water is removed and drying equipment is running, the more of the building can usually be kept. The emergency line is open 24 hours a day, and same-day response is offered when crews are available.

Do you handle water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Lima?

Yes. The emergency line is answered 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. While you wait, stop the water source if you can, shut off the main valve if you cannot find the leak, and keep power off to wet areas unless the panel is dry and safe to reach.

What is the difference between water damage restoration and water damage repair?

Restoration is the emergency and drying phase. It covers water extraction, moisture mapping, and running air movers and dehumidifiers until materials read dry. Repair is the rebuild that follows, such as replacing drywall, flooring and trim. Many Lima losses need both, and the drying readings decide how much repair is needed.

How long does drying take?

Most structures need several days of drying, and dense materials like hardwood, plaster and subfloors can take longer. The only reliable answer comes from moisture readings taken each day. Equipment stays until the readings match dry reference points, not until a calendar date passes.

What about a large loss with several rooms or floors affected?

Large losses are mapped room by room with moisture meters and thermal imaging so the scope is written down before demolition starts. That written scope also becomes your documentation for the insurance claim. If the loss involves a commercial space, see the commercial water damage page for how multi-unit and business losses are handled.

Do you waterproof basements?

No. Water damage restoration cleans up and dries what a leak or flood has already wet. Waterproofing and foundation repair are different trades that stop water from entering in the first place. If water keeps returning to your basement, we can document the damage and point you toward the right specialist, then handle the cleanup in our basement water damage cleanup work.
