Do You Need a Permit to Repair Water Damage in Lima, OH?

Most drying work needs no permit, but some repairs do. Here is who handles what in Lima.

By the Lima Water Damage Pros team · 2026

Whether you need a permit to repair water damage in Lima depends on what is being repaired. Drying and cleanup generally do not require one, while replacing wiring, plumbing or structural parts often does. This guide explains who handles which permits locally and what to ask your contractor.

We are not a permitting authority, and rules change. Always confirm with the office that handles your address before work begins.

Drying Usually Does Not Need A Permit

Extraction, equipment placement and routine drying are mitigation tasks, and they are not typically permitted work. The permits come in when the building is altered.

Replacing drywall or flooring may or may not need a permit depending on the scope. Ask the building department before assuming either way.

Permits Outside City Limits

Properties outside Lima may fall under different authorities. We do not have verified details for each nearby town, and we do not guess.

Call the village or city office, or the county, for your address. Our pages for Spencerville and Ottawa list the towns we serve.

Questions For Your Contractor

  1. Is a permit needed for this scope?
  2. Who pulls it, you or me?
  3. What inspections are required?
  4. Is the electrician or plumber licensed for this work?
  5. Will the permit and inspection dates delay the schedule?

A contractor who pulls permits where needed protects you at resale and in an insurance claim.

Why It Matters For Insurance And Resale

Unpermitted work can complicate an insurance claim and a future sale. Buyers and lenders sometimes ask for documentation of repairs after a known loss.

Keep permit records with your claim file. Related reading on the repair side is on the wall and drywall water damage repair page.

If The Repair Involves A Water Heater Or Plumbing

Replacing a water heater or a supply line is plumbing work. Ask the plumber who pulls the permit and whether an inspection is required.

The water heater leak cleanup page covers the cleanup that follows a failed unit.

Permit Timing And Rebuild Schedules

Permits can add days to a rebuild, so ask about them early. If drywall or electrical work needs approval, plan the schedule around inspections.

Drying should not wait for permits. Mitigation usually starts at once, and permits matter for the later repair phase.

Ask your contractor to build permit steps into the schedule and tell you when each inspection is expected.

What An Inspection Looks For

An inspector checks that work matches code and the permit. For electrical work, they look at wiring, boxes and protection. For plumbing, they check connections and pressure.

Be present or available during inspections if you can, and keep the paperwork afterward. A passed inspection is a record that helps when you sell.

If a repair fails inspection, the contractor fixes the issue and schedules another visit. Ask who pays for re-inspection before work starts.

Common Repair Jobs And Whether They May Need Approval

Replacing a few sheets of drywall and repainting is often treated as minor work. Replacing a ceiling, rebuilding a wall or altering framing is more likely to need review.

Swapping a wet outlet or rewiring a room is electrical work and usually requires a licensed electrician and a permit. Replacing a water heater or moving plumbing is plumbing work with its own process.

Flooring replacement is usually not permitted work by itself, but a subfloor repair can be. When in doubt, call before you start.

Write down the name of the person you spoke to and the date. If a question comes up later, that note is useful.

A Short Permit Checklist

Before the rebuild starts, write down the scope, list the trades involved and call the offices that handle each one. Ask what is required and what it costs.

Then ask your contractor to confirm in writing who is pulling each permit and when inspections will happen. A one page note is enough, and it prevents confusion when the schedule gets busy.

Keep it with the invoices.

Calling The Right Lima Office

For Lima addresses, a notice reported by local media puts building, electrical and HVAC permits with the city and plumbing permits with the county health department. When in doubt, call both offices before work starts and write down who you spoke to.

Rules for historic downtown buildings can differ, so owners of older properties near the courthouse should ask about any additional requirements.

Questions About This Topic

Do I need a permit to replace drywall?

It depends on the scope and local rules. Ask the building department.

Who pulls the permit, me or the contractor?

It varies. Settle it in writing before work starts.

Is a permit needed for water extraction?

Generally no. Extraction and drying are not permitted work in most cases.

What if I hire someone who does not pull permits?

You may face problems at resale or with insurance. Ask first.

Does Allen County handle all my permits?

Not all. Plumbing goes through the health department, and building, electrical and HVAC through the city for Lima addresses.
