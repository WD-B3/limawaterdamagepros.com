Before The Crew Arrives

Shut off the water

Find the main valve and close it if you cannot stop the leak at the fixture.

Mind the wiring

Stay out of water that touches outlets or the panel, and ask the utility if unsure.

Raise valuables

Lift furniture, papers and electronics above the wet area.

Take photos

Record the water line and affected rooms before cleanup begins.

Getting The Details Right For Your Claim

Water losses involve documentation as much as drying. We take photographs and daily moisture readings, and the written scope lists the rooms, materials and equipment.

Questions To Ask Any Restoration Company

Ask whether the crew follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and ask for a written scope and price before work starts. Ask how moisture will be measured and how often readings will be shared with you.

A company that cannot explain its drying plan, or that offers a firm price over the phone without seeing the loss, is guessing. We explain the scope first, and we ask you to compare it with any other estimate you receive. Our structural drying and dehumidification page shows what a good drying plan includes.
