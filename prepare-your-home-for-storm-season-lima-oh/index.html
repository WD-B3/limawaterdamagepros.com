Prepare Your Lima, OH Home for Storm Season

A few hours of prep can keep heavy rain from becoming water damage. Here is a practical checklist.

By the Lima Water Damage Pros team · 2026

Storm season in northwest Ohio brings heavy rain, wind and sometimes flooding. Preparation reduces the chance that rain turns into water damage, and it makes recovery easier if it does. This guide lists practical steps for Lima homeowners.

Nothing here guarantees a dry house. The goal is to lower the odds and be ready if water gets in.

Outside The House

  1. Clean gutters and downspouts and extend downspouts away from the foundation.
  2. Check grading so soil slopes away from the walls.
  3. Clear window wells and make sure covers fit.
  4. Inspect roof flashing, vents and shingles for damage.
  5. Trim branches that could fall on the roof.

Roof and ceiling problems from storms are explained on the storm water damage cleanup page.

Inside The House

Test the sump pump by pouring water into the pit. Consider a battery backup, because storms often come with power outages.

Raise stored items off basement floors, and keep important papers in waterproof containers. Know where the main water shutoff and electrical panel are.

Sewer And Drain Backup

Lima has a mix of combined and separate sewers, and heavy rain can overload drains. Ask a plumber about a backwater valve, especially if your basement has floor drains.

Ask your insurer whether sewer backup is covered. If a backup happens, the sewage backup cleanup page explains what to do.

Document Your Home Now

Photograph each room, the basement and the exterior before storm season. These dated photos show the before state if you file a claim.

Review your policy for water, wind and sewer backup. The flood damage restoration page explains why flood usually needs its own coverage.

After The Storm

Stay out of rooms with standing water near electrical equipment. Photograph damage before cleanup, and call a crew if water is more than a small spill.

Do not enter a basement with rising water. Call the utility if you are unsure about power.

Sump Pumps And Backup Power

A sump pump moves water out of a basement pit, and it stops when the power does. A battery backup or a water-powered backup keeps it working during outages.

Test the pump twice a year by pouring a bucket of water into the pit. Check that the discharge line runs well away from the foundation.

Replace old pumps before they fail. A pump usually fails during the storm that needs it most.

A Plan For The Household

Agree on who shuts off the water, who calls whom and where everyone goes if the basement floods. A written plan is easier to follow in the dark.

Teach everyone to stay out of water near electrical equipment. Children and guests may not think of it.

Keep your insurance information, policy number and agent contact in one place that does not depend on power or internet.

Basement Prep That Pays Off

Move stored items onto shelves or pallets, and avoid keeping cardboard boxes on the floor. Cardboard acts like a sponge and ruins everything inside.

Check the floor drain by pouring water into it and watching it flow away. Slow drains can back up during storms.

Seal obvious cracks in the foundation walls, but remember that sealing does not replace drainage. If water enters often, ask a waterproofing contractor for advice.

Photograph the basement while it is dry. The picture helps your claim and reminds you what was where.

A Final Pre-Storm Walk Through

The day before a forecast storm, walk the property with a short list. Check gutters, downspouts, the sump pump, the battery on your flashlight and your phone charge.

Move cars away from low spots and bring loose items indoors. If heavy rain is expected overnight, put sandbags or plastic where water has entered before.

Save the emergency number where everyone in the house can find it.

Review your plan each spring and again before winter. Replace flashlight batteries, test the sump pump and update your contact list, because details change from year to year and a plan that was current two years ago may not work now.

Local Weather Facts

Lima averages about 38 inches of precipitation a year based on the 1991 to 2020 normals, and local reporting in June 2015 described flooding in Lima after four to six inches of rain from the remnants of Tropical Storm Bill. Lima also keeps five reservoirs holding about 15 billion gallons, filled by rain and by pumping from the Auglaize and Ottawa Rivers, according to a local TV report.

Questions About This Topic

When is storm season in Lima?

Heavy rain can arrive in spring and summer, and winter brings its own risks such as ice dams.

Do I need a sump pump?

It depends on your basement and water table. A plumber can advise.

Does insurance cover storm damage?

Wind and hail damage are often covered, while flood and sewer backup often are not.

What should I do if the basement floods?

Stay out if power may be wet, photograph the water and call a crew.

Can I prevent every leak?

No. Preparation lowers the risk but cannot remove it.
