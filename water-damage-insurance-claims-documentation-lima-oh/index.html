Documenting Water Damage for an Insurance Claim in Lima, OH

Good records make a claim easier to review. Here is what to collect, in what order and why.

By the Lima Water Damage Pros team · 2026

A water damage claim moves on evidence. Photographs, a list of damaged items, receipts and dated notes let the adjuster see what you saw. This guide covers what to collect in Lima, from the first hour to the last repair.

None of this requires special skills. It requires doing a few simple things before the cleanup starts, because once materials are removed the evidence is gone.

Take Photographs Before Anything Moves

Photograph every wet room from the doorway, then close up. Include the water line on walls, wet flooring, ceiling stains and the source of the water if you can see it.

Take wide shots and detail shots. Video with a verbal description also helps, especially if water is still moving.

Build A Timeline

  1. Write down when you discovered the water and what you saw.
  2. Note what you did to stop it, such as shutting off the main valve.
  3. Record the time you called your insurer and the name of the person you spoke to.
  4. Log each visit from a plumber, adjuster or restoration crew.
  5. Save every text, email and receipt in one folder.

List What Was Damaged

Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them or has told you in writing that you may discard them.

Moisture Readings And The Scope

A restoration crew takes moisture readings in walls and floors. Those readings show which materials were wet and when they dried. Ask for a copy of the daily readings and the written scope.

The scope lists rooms, materials and equipment. It is the document your adjuster compares with their own estimate, so make sure it matches what the crew actually did. Our structural drying page explains the readings in more detail.

Keep Receipts For Emergency Steps

Save receipts for emergency extraction, temporary repairs, hotel stays and cleaning supplies. Ask your insurer which of these it reimburses.

If the loss involved contaminated water, ask what documentation your insurer wants before removing carpet or drywall. The flood damage restoration page explains why such materials are usually removed.

Common Documentation Mistakes

Throwing away damaged materials before the adjuster has seen them is the most common mistake. Cleaning up before taking photographs is the second.

A third is leaving gaps in the timeline. Even a short note such as the date the stain first appeared helps the insurer decide whether a loss was sudden.

Working With The Adjuster

An adjuster reviews the loss and writes an estimate. Meet them at the property if you can, and walk through each room with your photographs and your timeline.

Share the restoration scope and the daily moisture readings. When your documents and theirs line up, there is less to argue about.

If the adjuster disagrees with part of the scope, ask for the reason in writing. Differences often come from missing readings or missing photographs, and both can be supplied.

Keeping Records Organized

Create one folder, digital or paper, for the claim. Put in photographs, the timeline, receipts, the scope, readings, emails and the claim number.

Back up the digital folder in a second place. A phone that gets dropped or a flooded desk should not erase your evidence.

When the claim closes, keep the folder. It supports future claims, helps with resale and shows what was repaired and when.

Special Documentation For Contaminated Water

If sewage or flood water was involved, photograph the materials before they are removed. Porous materials soaked by that water are usually removed, and the photographs show why.

Ask the crew to note the water category on the scope and to record the readings of surrounding materials.

Keep waste disposal receipts if you paid for them separately. Some policies pay for disposal.

Share everything with your adjuster promptly, because contaminated materials leave the site quickly.

A Weekly Habit That Helps

If your claim stays open for weeks, spend a few minutes each week updating your folder. Add new photographs, receipts and notes about calls.

Review the scope against the work you can see. If something looks different, raise it while the crew is still on site.

A tidy file makes the final settlement conversation shorter.

Records For Lima Properties

Flooding is one of the hazards in Allen County's 2020 Hazard Mitigation Plan, and the June 2015 storm hit both Lima and Ada. If you live in a low-lying part of the county, keep a dated photograph of your basement and mechanical room in dry condition, so you can show the before state.

Questions About This Topic

How many photos should I take?

More than you think you need. Wide shots of each room and close-ups of each damaged item are a good start.

Can I start cleanup before the adjuster arrives?

Take emergency steps to stop further damage, but photograph first and keep damaged materials unless they are unsafe.

Should I keep the failed part?

Yes. A burst hose or cracked fitting helps show what failed.

Do I need an itemized scope?

It makes the claim easier to review. Ask the restoration company for one.

Who do I call if the claim is denied?

Start with your insurer's appeal process. The state insurance department's consumer line, 800-686-1526, can explain your options.
