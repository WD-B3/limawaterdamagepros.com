Sudden Jump in Your Water Bill in Lima, OH: What to Check

A higher bill can mean a hidden leak. Here is a simple way to check before water damage shows up.

By the Lima Water Damage Pros team · 2026

A sudden increase in a water bill is one of the earliest signs of a hidden leak. Many leaks run for weeks before they stain a ceiling or soften a floor. This guide shows a simple check you can run at home in Lima.

If you find a leak, the plumber fixes it, and a restoration crew dries what got wet. Finding it early keeps the second job small.

Compare The Bill With Last Year

Look at the same months last year, since use changes with season and guests. A jump with no change in routine is worth checking.

Check the bill for unusual usage dates. A jump in one billing period points to a recent change, such as a failed toilet flapper or a new irrigation system.

The Meter Test

  1. Turn off every faucet, appliance and irrigation system.
  2. Find the water meter and note the reading or watch the small leak indicator.
  3. Wait one to two hours without using water.
  4. Read the meter again.
  5. If it moved, water is going somewhere.

If the meter moves, close the main valve and see whether it stops. If it stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak may be in the service line.

What To Do When You Find Wet Material

If a leak has wet drywall, flooring or a ceiling, stop the water and have the materials dried. A leak that has run for weeks has often wet more than you can see.

Moisture meters show whether the area is wet or dry. Photograph the damage before repair, since insurers treat long-term leaks differently from sudden ones.

Who To Call First

Call a plumber to find and fix the leak. Call a restoration crew if there is wet material to dry. If you are not sure which, describe the symptoms and ask.

Water bills sometimes allow adjustments after a leak. Ask your utility about any policy for leak-related adjustments.

Preventing Future Surprises

Check the meter every few months. Replace old rubber supply lines. Know where your main shutoff is and test it once a year.

Leak sensors near the water heater, washer and sinks give an early alarm.

Reading Your Meter Properly

Most meters show a flow indicator, often a small dial or triangle, that spins when any water is moving. If it spins when everything is off, you have a leak.

Write down the register reading, wait a few hours and check again. The difference tells you how much water escaped.

Take a photograph of the meter each time. It gives you proof for the utility and for your insurer.

Why Small Leaks Turn Into Damage

A leak the size of a pencil lead can release a lot of water over weeks. The water finds drywall, insulation and flooring and soaks them quietly.

By the time a stain shows, the hidden area is usually larger than the visible one. That is why moisture readings matter after a repair.

Fixing the pipe and drying the structure are separate jobs. The plumber handles the first, and a restoration crew handles the second.

Talking To The Utility

Call the water utility and ask whether the meter was read correctly and whether the bill reflects an estimate. Meter errors happen occasionally.

Ask about any program for leak adjustments and what documentation they need, such as a plumber invoice. Policies differ, so ask before you assume.

Keep a copy of the bill and your meter photographs. If the bill is wrong, you will need them to dispute it.

If the leak was inside the home and damaged materials, call a restoration crew after the plumber so the wet area can be dried.

Steps If You Cannot Find The Leak

If the meter moves and you cannot find the source, close the main valve and call a plumber. Describe the meter test results so they know where to start.

A plumber can use listening devices and pressure tests to find hidden leaks. After the repair, check the meter again to confirm it is quiet.

Then have any wet materials dried before they are closed up.

Lima Weather And Your Bill

Lima averages about 38 inches of precipitation a year, so lawn irrigation is optional for much of the season. A spring or summer bill that spikes without a dry spell is a clue to a leak.

In winter, a frozen line can split quietly. Read about it on the frozen pipe water damage page, and see our local notes for Spencerville and Wapakoneta.

Questions About This Topic

How much could a toilet leak cost me?

A running toilet can waste a lot of water over a month. Check the flapper first.

Does my insurance cover a high water bill?

Generally not. It may cover resulting damage if the leak was sudden.

Can a leak be outside the house?

Yes. A service line leak outside can raise your bill without any wet floors.

How do I know if it is a slab leak?

Warm floor spots, a hissing sound and a rising bill suggest one. A plumber can confirm.

Should I turn off the water at night?

If you suspect a leak, yes. Close the main valve until a plumber checks.
